# Feelio's Adventures: SEL scenarios design (PROPOSAL, 2026-10-19)

> **Status: proposal for the owner. Nothing here is built, and no scenario content, assets or code were created.** Every scenario still needs writing, expert review (SPEC §5.3) and approval before it reaches a child.

## 0. In one paragraph
Feelio has just arrived in our world from the world of feelings, and it is still learning how feelings work here. **The child isn't the student. The child is Feelio's guide.** Each level is a small living scene (a diorama) where Feelio and its friends run into a real everyday moment. The child helps by touching the world: calming Feelio's stormy screen, peeking into a friend's thoughts, trying an action and watching what happens, then **rewinding to try another**. Nothing is ever wrong; some things just turn out differently. What the child learns isn't a sentence at the end. It's a **tool they keep** (the Cloud Breath, the Thought Peek, the Feeling Dial…), and it becomes a real power in later levels. The world itself shows progress: each place's "feelings weather" clears as the child helps.

## 1. What the research says, and what we take from it
| Finding | Source | What we do with it |
|---|---|---|
| SEL works best when it is **SAFE**: Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit. Programs that met all four had larger effects across outcomes (213 programs). | Durlak et al. 2011, via [CASEL slides](https://casite-809548.cloudaccess.net/images/CASEL-Meta-analysis-slides-for-MoR-1.pdf) and [EdWeek](https://edweek.org/ew/articles/2011/02/04/20sel.h30.html) | The levels are a sequence (§3), the child is always doing something (§2), and every level has one focused skill. | CASEL's five competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making. | [CASEL framework](https://casel.org/framework/) | Each place leans on one or two competencies, in that order of growing complexity (§3). |
| Microsoft Reflect's **Feelings Monster** builds emotional vocabulary with a character whose face and body show the feeling (over 50 emotions), with a simpler vocabulary setting for younger children. | [Microsoft Design: the Feelings Monster](https://microsoft.design/articles/the-feelings-monster-building-a-character-with-all-the-feels/), [Reflect](https://reflect.microsoft.com/) | Feelio already is a feelings character with a screen for a face. Its **Heart Screen** becomes the visual language for feelings everywhere (§2.1). Vocabulary grows with age (4 words, then about 12, then 20+). Note: Microsoft calls it research-backed but doesn't name the studies. |
| Emotion vocabulary ("granularity") is the core of Yale's RULER, but independent trials with children are sparse. | [QUB](https://qub.ac.uk/courses/postgraduate-research/phd-opportunities/words-for-feelings-exploring-the-development-of-emotional-granularity-in-children-and-adolescents.html), [RULER](https://rulerapproach.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/RULER_Brochure.pdf) | We use feeling *size* and *mix* (the Feeling Dial) rather than word drills, and we don't claim it changes wellbeing. |

| Children can use cognitive regulation (reappraisal, distraction) from about 5–6. At 10–12 they pick reappraisal for small feelings and distraction for big ones. | [JoVE methods paper](https://www.jove.com/t/55200), [Haifa study](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/flexible-emotion-regulatory-selection-in-1012-year-old-children-c/) | Tools include both "look again" (reappraisal: the Flashlight) and "do something else" (distraction: the Wait Wizard). | Children who played the *Daniel Tiger* app used the show's regulation strategies more, a month later. Distance makes hard feelings safe to explore, and keeps the child's own private feelings out of the game. |
| Teaching a character (the "protégé effect") increased effort and learning, especially for children who struggle, and protected their egos from failure. | [Chase et al. 2009](https://purl.stanford.edu/zm369yx3532) | **The child is Feelio's guide.** Feelio tries things, and the child helps it. When something goes badly it's Feelio's attempt, not the child's failure. |
| Play-based learning is joyful, meaningful, actively engaging, iterative and socially interactive. | [LEGO Foundation](https://cms.learningthroughplay.com/media/vd5fiurk/what-we-mean-by-learning-through-play.pdf) | **Rewind** makes it iterative. | The evidence for SEL games in general is promising but thin and uneven. | [2024 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10931397/), [Zheng et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/simgam/v52y2021i6p686-714.html) | **We make no claims of effect.** We plan a real pilot (§6.3). |

## 2. The core toys (reused in every level)
The trap in this genre is a "situation → 3 answer cards → feedback text" loop. That's a quiz with pictures: it feels like a test, it needs reading, and it's predictable after two levels. Instead, five **toys** with real cause and effect, each reused across many levels. Reuse is also what keeps the art budget sane.

### 2.1 The Heart Screen (self-awareness)
Feelio's screen-face shows its inner weather: sunny, drizzly, stormy, foggy (worried), fizzy (excited), and two at once for mixed feelings. Weather layers are approved overlay assets, not drawn by code.
- **Feeling Dial:** the child turns a big dial to find the *size* of a feeling ("a little grumpy" vs "very grumpy"). The weather and Feelio's pose follow it.
- **Body clues:** tapping Feelio's body reveals clues (fists, drooping antenna, fast heart flashing on the belly heart). This is where feelings live in the body.

### 2.2 The storm narrows the choices (self-management): the signature mechanic
When Feelio is stormy, **only one or two action cards are visible, and they're the impulsive ones** (stomp, shout). As the child helps Feelio calm down with a tool, the storm clears and **more cards appear**, one by one. The child *sees* that big feelings shrink our options, and calming down gives them back. No one says "calm down first"; the screen shows it.

### 2.3 Rewind (responsible decision-making)
Every action plays out as a short animated consequence in the diorama: the cousin's face falls, the tower can't be rebuilt, a friend walks over. Then a big rewind (a tape-spin animation) returns to the choice. **Every consequence the child discovers is collected as a page in Feelio's Journal**, so curiosity, not punishment, drives them to explore the "bad" branches too. Comparing the branches is the learning. Consequences are always social and mild (how others feel, what's possible next), never punishment or shame.

### 2.4 Thought Peek and Swap Shoes (social awareness)
Tap another character to peek into their thought bubble, as a picture, not words. It reveals hidden reasons: he's quiet because he's tired, not angry. Later, **Swap Shoes** replays the same moment from the other character's side, split-screen. This targets the belief-based and hidden-emotion understanding that develops between 6 and 10.

### 2.5 The Calm Kit (strategies as powers)
Earned in play, kept forever, usable in every later level. The breathing, noticing and stretching games are already built in Talk, so we reuse them:
| Tool | What it is | Earned in |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling Dial | Size a feeling | Home 1 |
| Cloud Breath | The breathing game | Home 3 |
| Thought Peek | See why someone acts this way | Home 2 |
| Flashlight | Look again: reappraisal | Home 4 |
| Plan B Spinner | Flexible thinking when plans break | Home 5 |
| Wait Wizard | Distraction for waiting | Park 1 |
| Listening Ears | Listen before helping | Park 5 |
| Swap Shoes | Full perspective switch | School 5 |
| Kind Words tiles | Build "I feel… when… can we…" | School 5 / Club |

From Park on, **the child chooses which tool to use**. That choice is how we see learning carry over (§6.2).

## 3. The journey: five places, rising emotional complexity
Follows the map's unlock order and the developmental order from §1.

| Place | Theme | Main competencies | Emotion understanding (TEC) | New toy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Home | "Feelio's own feelings" | Self-awareness, first regulation | Recognising, outside causes, wishes | Heart Screen, Dial, Cloud Breath, Flashlight |
| 2 Park | "Big feelings with other kids around" | Self-management, early empathy | Regulation strategies | Storm narrows choices, Rewind, Wait Wizard |
| 3 School | "Other minds" | Social awareness, relationships | Beliefs and misunderstandings, reminders | Thought Peek, Swap Shoes |
| 4 Club | "Groups and mixed feelings" | Relationship skills | Hidden feelings, mixed feelings | Two-weather screen, group mood |
| 5 Market | "The wider world and doing right" | Responsible decision-making | Moral feelings (guilt vs shame) | Dilemmas with trade-offs |

Pacing: every place has one **rest level** (short, calm, meditative) between harder ones, plus a **festival finale** at the end of Club that brings back everyone met so far (§4.4 Club 5).

## 4. Scenario concepts (25 levels, matched to the existing level titles)
Format: **concept**, then the *child discovers* (never told), then interaction and animation, then age layers where they matter. The **signature** levels, marked ★, get the most polish and make the case for the whole design.

### 4.1 Home: Feelio's own feelings
1. **Leaving for school → "The Wobbly Morning."** Feelio's screen drizzles and its antenna droops. The child becomes a feelings detective: tapping clues in the room (a lost shoe, rain on the window, a drawing it's proud of) reveals *why*. Then they turn the Feeling Dial to match.
   - *Discovers:* feelings have causes and sizes.
   - Earns the **Feeling Dial**.
2. **Family talks at the table → "The Quiet Dinner."** An adult at the table is silent; Feelio's screen fogs: "Is she cross with me?" A Thought Peek shows she's just tired from work.
   - *Discovers:* what we guess isn't always what's true.
   - Earns the **Thought Peek**.
3. **★ Sharing with a brother or sister → "The Tower."** Feelio and its little cousin build a tower; the cousin knocks it down. Stormy screen, and only two cards: Stomp, Shout. Both play out (the cousin cries, the blocks scatter further); rewind. Then the cushion corner glows: the **Cloud Breath** (the existing breathing game) clears the storm, and new cards appear: "Build it again together", "Tell how I feel". The ending: the cousin hands Feelio a block.
   - *Discovers:* every feeling is okay; calming down gives you more choices.
   - Earns the **Cloud Breath**.
4. **Bedtime worries → "Shadows on the Wall."** Gentle, never scary. A shape on the wall looks like a monster; the child drags a flashlight to look again (a coat on a hook). Feelio's fog shrinks. At the end Feelio puts any left-over worry in a jar "to tell a grown-up in the morning".
   - *Discovers:* looking again can change a feeling, and worries can be shared with grown-ups.
   - Earns the **Flashlight**.
5. **Going out to play → "Rainy Day Plan B."** Rain cancels the park. The child spins a Plan B wheel to build a blanket fort with what's in the room.
   - *Discovers:* disappointment passes when you find another way.
   - Rest level. Earns the **Plan B Spinner**.

### 4.2 Park: big feelings with other kids around
1. **★ Waiting for a turn → "The Swing Line."** A real-time wait: the swing timer feels endless. Pushing in plays out (the other kid falls off, upset); rewind. The **Wait Wizard** offers things to do while waiting: count the clouds, spot the ducks (a variant of the existing noticing game). The wait visibly "shrinks".
   - *Discovers:* waiting feels shorter when you're busy.
   - 10–12: chooses between a distraction and a "look again" thought ("my turn is next").
2. **Calming down by the pond → "The Mirror Pond."** Rest level. The pond ripples with Feelio's feelings; slow breaths still the water until Feelio's reflection is clear.
   - *Discovers:* a calm mind sees clearly (a metaphor carried by animation alone).
3. **Losing a game → "The Snail Race."** Feelio's snail loses to a friend's. Sulk, "You cheated!" or "Good race!" each play out.
   - 10–12: a two-weather screen (disappointed *and* happy for a friend).
   - *Discovers:* you can lose and still be a good friend.
4. **Sharing a picnic → "Three Friends, Two Cookies."** A fairness sandbox: drag, split, trade, take turns. **Several fair answers are celebrated**; only grabbing makes faces fall.
   - *Discovers:* fairness can be solved many ways.
5. **Helping someone who is sad → "The Kid on the Bench."** The child chooses how Feelio comes close (too fast, beside, far away) and what it does first (give advice, offer a toy, sit and listen). The kid only opens up when Feelio listens first.
   - *Discovers:* comfort starts with listening.
   - Earns **Listening Ears**.

### 4.3 School: other minds
1. **The first day → "Two Weathers."** Feelio's screen shows nervous fog *and* excited fizz at once. The child finds what feeds each one.
   - *Discovers:* two feelings can happen together. The mixed-feelings concept is introduced here and deepened in Club.
2. **Nervous about a test → "Thought Balloons."** Worry thoughts float up as dark balloons. The child taps each one to find a *realistic* helpful thought ("I practised some. I can try."), never toxic positivity ("I'll get 100%"). The belly heart slows.
   - *Discovers:* thoughts change feelings.
   - 5–6: body clues and the Cloud Breath only, no thought work.
3. **Feeling left out → "The Chalk Game."** Kids play and Feelio isn't invited. Thought Peek: they didn't see Feelio (a belief, not rejection). Options: ask to join, find another friend, or feel sad and tell a grown-up if it keeps happening.
   - *Unlocked replay:* Feelio later notices **another** kid left out, and the child helps include them (going from bystander to someone who helps).
4. **Making a friend → "Conversation Catch."** Talking is a game of catch: questions are balls. Hogging the ball (talking only about yourself) stalls the game; throwing it back keeps it going.
   - *Discovers:* friendship is back-and-forth.
5. **★ Arguing with a friend → "The Broken Crayon."** Both kids are sure the other broke it. **Swap Shoes** replays the moment from both sides, split-screen, and reveals the misunderstanding. The repair needs two pieces, an apology and a fix, built with Kind Words tiles.
   - *Discovers:* both sides can feel wronged; repairing beats winning.
   - Earns **Swap Shoes** and **Kind Words**.

### 4.4 Club: groups and mixed feelings
1. **Joining a group → "Joining the Fort."** Rushing in and changing the game fails; watching first, then joining what they're already doing, works. This follows peer-entry research, so the "obvious" answer isn't the right one, which keeps the level interesting.
2. **Taking turns in music → "The Band."** A rhythm game where Feelio must leave space for the others; playing over them turns the music to noise.
   - *Discovers:* listening is part of playing together.
   - Joy level.
3. **Hearing feedback on art → "Feelio's Painting."** The teacher suggests a change; Feelio's screen sinks. Thought Peek: she likes it and wants to help.
   - *Discovers:* feedback is about the work, not about you. Older children practise asking a question back.
4. **Losing as a team → "The Team Mood."** A group mood meter: blaming one player drops everyone's weather; encouraging lifts it.
   - *Discovers:* feelings spread through a group.
5. **★ Stage fright → "The Festival."** The capstone. Feelio must sing at the festival, and **every tool returns**: body clues, the Cloud Breath, a thought balloon, a friend's support. The audience is everyone met so far (the cousin, the bench kid, the crayon friend), each reacting.
   - *Discovers:* you already have what you need.
   - Unlocks a special room item.

### 4.5 Market: the wider world, doing right
1. **Waiting in line → "The Tired Cashier."** A grumpy grown-up; Thought Peek shows she's been standing all day.
   - *Discovers:* grown-ups have feelings too; kindness changes the mood.
2. **Wanting something you can't have → "The Shiny Kite."** Disappointment and waiting for later. The child adds the kite to a wish list; the wanting feeling shrinks over time (animated).
   - *Discovers:* wanting is a feeling that passes.
3. **Feeling lost → "Where Did Everyone Go?"** **Safety content:** stay where you are, look for a helper (a staff badge or a parent with children), say your grown-up's name.
   - The rules come from per-market configuration and need expert and safety review.
   - Never scary; Feelio stays calm.
4. **Being honest → "The Cracked Jar."** Feelio bumps a jar. Hide it (Feelio's screen stays heavy, the shopkeeper finds out anyway), or tell (the shopkeeper appreciates it, and they fix it together).
   - *Discovers:* guilt is a feeling that points to repair (guilt, not shame: the language is always about the action, never "you're bad").
5. **Being kind → "Little Kindnesses."** An open sandbox: the child notices needs around the market (dropped apples, a lost glove) and helps. Each kindness brightens the market. No count, no score.
   - Finale of the base world.

## 5. Mascot, animation and assets
### 5.1 How animation carries meaning
Every animation has a job, and none is only decoration:
| Animation | What it shows the child |
|---|---|
| Screen weather | The feeling |
| Weather intensity | The feeling's size |
| Cards appearing as the storm clears | Calm widens choices |
| Rewind spin | It's okay to try again |
| Faces of other characters reacting | Consequences |
| The world getting brighter | Progress |

- **Method:** the same as today. Approved transparent PNG cutouts, animated in code with transforms (squash, tilt, bob), cross-fades between poses, and particles made from approved brand motifs. Feelio is never mirrored or redrawn.
- **Reduced motion** keeps every meaning: weather changes in one step, rewind becomes a fade.

### 5.2 Assets to request (owner generates via the asset protocol; Claude Code never draws)
1. **Mascot emotions** beyond today's set, made from the official mascot:
   - worried, scared, embarrassed, frustrated, disappointed, proud, guilty-but-hopeful, tired, curious, "listening beside"
   - the **Heart Screen weather layers** (sun, drizzle, storm, fog, fizz, two-weather)
   - About 16 images.
2. **Supporting cast** (ART_DIRECTION §9, design once, then reuse): 4 children of different looks and ages, plus 2 adults, each with 8 expressions and 2–3 action poses. About 60 images, after one approved cast sheet.
3. **Dioramas:** 1–2 reusable backdrops per place (10) and a props sheet per place (about 25 props).
4. **Effects and tools:** thought-bubble frames, rewind, journal pages, the 9 tool icons. About 20.
5. **Total:** about 130 images for all 25 levels. **The vertical slice (§7) needs about 30.**

### 5.3 Voice and words
- **Minimal reading.** All lines are pre-recorded (rule 11): Feelio's voice, plus at most two cast voices, so each line can be told apart by ear.
- **Audio budget:** about 900–1,200 characters per level, so roughly 25–30k credits for all 25 levels in English. That's far less than full narration, because most of the meaning is visual. Arabic waits for an Arabic voice (captions until then).
- The vertical slice needs about 4k credits.

## 6. Progression without tests, and how we know it helps
### 6.1 Progression that never feels like a test
- **No scores, stars for correctness, timers or "wrong".** A level is done when the story reaches an ending; *any* ending counts. Exploring more endings fills Feelio's Journal (optional, curiosity-driven).
- **Progress the child can see:** the place's weather clears, tools fill the Calm Kit, the cast remembers you, and room items unlock (the existing system, never taken away).
- **Difficulty rises by complexity, not by pressure:** more characters, hidden reasons, mixed feelings, trade-offs. Not by speed or by punishing mistakes.
- **Age layers on the same story** (keeps art and review costs down):
  - **5–6:** 2 choices, audio only, body clues and calming tools.
  - **7–9:** 3 choices, Thought Peek.
  - **10–12:** dilemmas where every option has a trade-off, mixed and hidden feelings, choosing *which* tool fits, Kind Words sentences.

### 6.2 Learning signals from play (stealth assessment, Zoo U style)
Computed on the server as **counts and categories only**, never the child's words. Each needs a retention period and a parent-visibility decision from you before it's built (CLAUDE.md: ask before changing what parents see).
| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Uses a calming tool **before** being prompted, in later levels | Self-management carrying over |
| Uses the Feeling Dial at middle sizes, not just extremes | Granularity |
| Opens a Thought Peek before choosing | Perspective-taking habit |
| Explores more than one branch, then picks the social one | Considering consequences |
| In 10–12 dilemmas, picks a tool that fits the feeling's size (distraction for big feelings, look-again for small) | Flexible regulation, as in the Haifa study |

### 6.3 Real evaluation (needed before any claim)
1. **Expert review of every scenario** (an SEL specialist or child psychologist, plus a cultural reviewer for Arabic). This is already required: `review_status`.
2. **Playtests with children** (consented, parent present, no data kept): 5–8 children per age band, playing while saying what they think, with the Fun Toolkit "smileyometer" for enjoyment. Test: do they understand the toys without text? Do they rewind on their own?
3. **Pilot with families** (needs proper plans and data agreements, per CLAUDE.md, before real child data):
   - before, after, and **a one-month follow-up**, because the Daniel Tiger effect showed up only after a month
   - a validated parent rating, such as the [DESSA-mini](https://www.hogrefe.com/uk/shop/tests/ability-achievement/devereux-student-strengths-assessment-mini.html) (8 items, ages 5–14, made for progress monitoring; licensed), and/or an emotion-understanding measure (TEC) run by a researcher
   - a comparison group (wait-list)
4. **Healthy-engagement checks:** replays are chosen, not compelled; sessions stay within the parent's limits; no pattern of compulsive play.
5. **Until then:** no "improves", "proven" or "research-backed" claims in the product or marketing. We write "designed with SEL research in mind; not yet evaluated".

### 6.4 The bridge to real life
- **Optional "Tell Feelio about a time…"** after a level opens Talk, already knowing the scenario (SPEC §4.1, context passed without personal data). Never required, and the game never asks the child to disclose their own feelings in writing.
- **Parent prompt** (dashboard, one line per finished level): "Today Feelio learned to wait for a turn with the Wait Wizard. Ask: what helps *you* wait?" This is where much of the Daniel Tiger effect came from.

## 7. Recommendation: build one slice first
Don't author 25 levels up front. Build a **vertical slice of three ★ levels**, one per core toy:
| Level | Proves |
|---|---|
| Home 3 "The Tower" | Heart Screen, storm narrows the choices, Rewind, Cloud Breath |
| Park 1 "The Swing Line" | A tool chosen and reused, a real-time feeling |
| School 5 "The Broken Crayon" | Thought Peek, Swap Shoes, Kind Words |

This means about 30 images, about 4k voice credits, one engine (the diorama player, branch and rewind, the Calm Kit, the Journal) and three scenario scripts for expert review. Then playtest with children, adjust, and only then write the rest. The scenario format in ARCHITECTURE §11 would grow to describe branches and toys; that's a design change to agree on before building.

## 8. Ideas considered and dropped
- **Quiz cards with "correct" answers:** a test in disguise, high reading load, and predictable. Replaced by toys and consequences.
- **Asking the child how *they* feel inside the game:** privacy and safety risk, and it breaks the safety of distance. The child helps characters; their own feelings belong to the optional Talk, with the safety layer.
- **Stars, streaks or timers for speed:** pressure, against CR-12.
- **AI-generated scenarios at play time:** SPEC says authored and reviewed only. AI may help draft offline.
- **Long animated cut-scenes:** passive, expensive, and children skip them. Every animation answers something the child did.
- **"Positive thinking" endings ("just be happy!"):** invalidating. Every feeling is okay; *actions* have consequences.

## 9. Open questions for the owner
1. Is the premise right: Feelio is new to our world, and the child is its guide?
2. Should the slice be the three ★ levels above?
3. Who reviews the content: an SEL specialist or child psychologist, plus an Arabic cultural reviewer?
4. Which in-game signals (§6.2), if any, should parents see? This changes what parents can see, so it's your decision.
5. Is the Market "Feeling lost" level in scope? It needs safety review and per-market rules.
